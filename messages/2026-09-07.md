## 2. Three questions about the cutoffs

The cutoffs are real. We can see them in our own recordings at the joins between sentences, and
they are at the top of the list this week. Three quick questions that would narrow it down:

1. When it cuts off, does a red message appear in the chat saying "Audio unavailable" or
   "Playback incomplete", or does it just go quiet with no message?
2. Is the "Hands-free interrupt" switch on or off when it happens?
3. Which phone or computer and which browser were you using?

Also: the "slow playback" report has two causes we found. Replaying your own recording downloads a
very large file every time, and a long rehearsal waits for the whole transcription with no progress
shown. Both are being fixed.

## 3. What material would help, in priority order

1. The course material for the class you just started: slides, handouts and exercise descriptions.
   That is the only new material the class trial needs.
2. Coaching sessions with question-and-answer, the kind your two testers valued, where one person
   unpacks a real problem. Transcripts with speaker labels plus the original audio, best sound
   quality first. This is where the model has the most to gain.
3. One recording of you talking conversationally for five to ten minutes, close to the microphone,
   with nobody else speaking. The tester who found the voice "recorded" knows your WhatsApp voice,
   and the voice currently copies a lesson recording. This is a candidate reference, not training.
4. The 30 to 60 second listening clip above, if you care about the video.

"How much is enough" gets answered by measurement: each batch is held out and the model is compared
with and without it, and we stop when it stops improving. Please do not send everything at once.
One complete, well-labelled session lets us validate the pipeline first. Older material is fine if
the sound is clean and the other participants have agreed to this use. Skip sessions with people
talking over each other or poor audio.
